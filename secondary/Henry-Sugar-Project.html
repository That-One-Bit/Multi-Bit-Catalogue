The Frames of Henry Sugar's Story

Finally, Imhrat Khan's story is complete. Not following the rules of the Yogis, he was found dead in his sleep. This was a shortened version of my findings. Ending, John Cartwright.' Henry finished the book. He knew that he was going to acquire these yoga powers for himself, just think of the money he could make! He was referring to the practice Imhrat tried: seeing the back face of a card. Henry happened to be one of the special people who could finish training in three years, leading him to concentrate on the task. This was the most dedicated he had ever been, eventually getting his (successful!) time down to four seconds. He tested his luck at his favorite casino, the Lords house. He had done it, but he didn't feel good about. There was no thrill, no risk, no... anything. Because of this, and many other factors that must be skipped, he decides to travel across the world. He donates money he earns at casinos in the places he visits, having his previous bank manager handle the winnings.

The story begins with Henry Sugar walking into frame. Henry is a greedy man, who is staying with his friends at Sir William Wyndham's mansion. They frequently play games such as roulette, blackjack, and canasta to gain more money. Henry is not above cheating for his own gain.

As his friends play canasta (a three player game), he wanders into the library. As he searches, he looks at some of the books. 'Boring rubbish...' he tells himself. Once he is about to leave, he notices a small, blue exercise book that children would read in school. There was no title on the cover, and it was covered in dust. He flipped it open, reading '...THE MAN WHO COULD SEE WITHOUT HIS EYES...'...

John Cartwright, the author of this book, begins with his introduction. 'I, John Cartwright, am a surgeon at Bombay General Hospital. On the morning of... I was in the Doctors' Rest Room having a cup of tea. There were three other doctors with me, all having earned the tea break. There was a knock on the door. "Come in," I said.' The mysterious person told us the whimsical idea that he could see without his eyes, requesting that we bandage his head and make sure that there was no way for him to see. We followed with the request, having used dough, collodion, and bandage.

After this, he tells us about his show and performance. I decide to attend the performance that night. He shows many acts that would require sight, giving a great performance that the crowd enjoys; he is the star of the show afterall.

Once the show is over, I make my way to the backstage. There, the man is waiting with his assistant. I decide to ask him to come with me to dinner, to allow me to interview him on how he gained this extraordinary power. He starts: 'I am an Indian, a Hindu, and I was born in Akhnur, in Kashmir State, in 1905. My family is poor and my father worked as...'

A conjurer came to the school I attended at the age of thirteen, calling themself a ‘professor’. His tricks were very good, leading me to chase after him and acquire magical powers of my own. After leaving home, I find out that the conjurer is two hundred miles away, having me take a train to follow him. Eventually, I find him. I demand he teach me his ways, in which he does. Though, I realize that these tricks are really just tricks, no magic involved. Immediately the Professor is no longer my hero. I lost every bit of interest in the job, wishing for real magic powers. I finally found what I was looking for: yoga.

Yoga is a religious practice that involves many years of concentration, training, and work. Meditation, solitude, and a peaceful environment are all recommended to obtain yoga powers and become a Yogi. A Yogi is one who demonstrates yoga powers, but one who must follow strict rules.

1. A Yogi must not use yoga powers for their own gain
2. A Yogi must not share the secrets of yoga with an outsider
3. A Yogi must not use their powers to harm others

One who does not follow the above will die. I learned all of this from the great Banerjee and a Yogi of Hardwar, though I don’t believe in it.
